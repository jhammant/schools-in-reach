## Benchmarking

The DfE's [Financial Benchmarking and Insights Tool](https://financial-benchmarking-and-insights-tool.education.gov.uk/) (FBIT) is the official place to compare a school or trust with similar schools. School Money uses the same published returns and adds:

- every figure **per pupil** first, with the total, its year and its source;
- the five money statuses, worked out the same way for every school;
- council and constituency medians, so you can see a school in its local picture;
- pins, so you can compare up to six schools side by side.

## Free expert help from the DfE

School resource management advisers (SRMAs) are accredited sector experts who give schools and academy trusts peer-to-peer advice on using their revenue and capital resources. The DfE's SRMA programme also runs a free one-to-one mentoring programme for school business managers, school finance managers and chief financial and operating officers. Places are limited.

## Buying

The DfE's Get help buying for schools service helps schools and trusts with buying, and its buying for schools guidance lists DfE-approved buying options. It also offers free training and webinars.

## Energy

Energy contracts work differently from home energy. The DfE's guidance says:

- do not let your contracts expire: at the end of a contract, if no action is taken, you move to a higher 'deemed rate';
- compare quotes like for like, and see if you can buy through a DfE-approved buying option;
- Energy for schools lets state-funded schools and trusts switch to the same energy contract the DfE uses. The DfE estimates schools could save up to 36% on their energy bills.

## Talking to parents about the figures

Parents may see your school's status on School Money. If it shows a deficit or a tight reserve, it helps to say why and what the plan is. The [guide for parents](/guides/parents/) explains what the statuses mean and suggests kind questions they might ask, and the [guide for governors](/guides/governors/) covers recovery plans and licensed deficits.
